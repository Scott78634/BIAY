Episode 0: Welcome to Bible in a Year

[Warm, friendly tone]

Hello, and welcome to Bible in a Year with Scott Purcell.

I’m so glad you’re here! Whether you’re brand new to the Bible, returning after a long time, or simply looking for a fresh way to walk through Scripture, you’ve just taken a beautiful step. Thank you for joining me on this journey.

This is your introductory episode—Episode 0—so today I’ll introduce the podcast, explain the reading plan we’ll follow, and share why I’m so excited to read the Bible together every day.

Here’s the plan in a nutshell: Every single day, we’ll read three portions of Scripture—one from the Old Testament, one from the New Testament, and one from Psalms or Proverbs. By following this rhythm, we’ll cover the entire Bible—from Genesis to Revelation—in exactly one year.

Why this structure? Because it brings the richness and depth of God’s Word in a balanced, sustainable way. Many people start out eager to read the whole Bible in a year, only to hit a wall in the long lists of laws or genealogies and lose momentum. This plan is designed to help you avoid that. You’ll get variety every day—narrative, poetry, prophecy, letters, wisdom—while still moving steadily through the whole story of Scripture. You’ll experience both continuity and freshness, and that makes it much easier to stay with it all the way to the end.

The reading plan we’re using comes from Biblica.com, and I’m deeply grateful to them for making it freely available. It’s a proven, time-tested schedule that thousands of people have used successfully.

For our readings, I’ve chosen the Lexham English Bible (LEB). Here’s why I love it:

It’s a highly accurate, scholarly translation that still reads smoothly and naturally when spoken aloud—perfect for a podcast. You’ll notice it sounds a little different from the versions you may be used to, and that’s actually a good thing. For example, on Day 1 you’ll hear “vaulted dome” instead of the more familiar “expanse” or “firmament” in Genesis 1. I’ll be honest—I’m not personally thrilled with that particular choice—but overall, I find the LEB trustworthy, clear, and refreshing.

One feature I especially appreciate is how the LEB handles God’s personal name, Yahweh. Most translations replace “YHWH” with “LORD” in all capital letters. The LEB keeps it as “Yahweh.” So when we read Genesis 2:4, you’ll hear, “…in the day that Yahweh God made earth and heaven…” instead of “…LORD God.” That small difference makes it immediately obvious when Scripture is using God’s covenant name—and since this is an audio podcast, you’ll actually hear the distinction every time. It’s a beautiful reminder of who we’re reading about.

My hope is that any unfamiliar wording will invite you to slow down, reflect, and meditate on the text more deeply rather than just brushing past it. Let the differences draw you closer to the Word.
