The Plan: A Balanced Approach
In this podcast, we aren’t just reading from front to back. We are following a plan—sourced from our friends at Biblica.com—that covers the entire Bible in 365 days. Every day, we’ll tackle three distinct sections:

A selection from the Old Testament.

A passage from the New Testament.

A reading from the Wisdom books (Psalms or Proverbs).

The reason for this specific "triple-threat" approach is simple: momentum. We’ve all been there—starting a reading plan with great intentions in January, only to "hit a wall" in the middle of a difficult or slow-paced book. By blending the Old and New Testaments with the poetry of the Psalms, we maintain a sense of variety and continuity. It’s a balanced spiritual diet that keeps you engaged from Genesis all the way to Revelation.

The Translation: Why the LEB?
You’ll notice right away that I’m reading from the Lexham English Bible (LEB). It’s a scholarly translation that manages to be incredibly clear while staying very close to the original text.

It might sound a little different than what you’re used to. For example, in the Day 1 reading, the LEB uses the phrase "vaulted dome" where many translations say "expanse." While every translation has its quirks, I’ve chosen the LEB for its transparency—especially regarding the names of God.

Most English Bibles replace the divine name of God—YHWH—with the title "LORD" in all capital letters. The LEB breaks that tradition. When the Hebrew text uses God’s personal name, the LEB brings it right into the English: Yahweh. In Genesis 2, you’ll hear "...in the day that Yahweh God made earth and heaven," instead of "the LORD God." I love this because it makes God’s personal, covenantal name immediate and obvious, even in an audio format where you can't see the capitalization on a page. I hope these subtle differences prompt you to meditate more deeply on the text rather than just skimming over familiar phrases.
